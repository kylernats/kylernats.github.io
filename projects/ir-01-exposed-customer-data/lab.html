Lab IR-01 · Incident Response

Customer data left open to the internet

~45 minutesAWS · S3 · CloudTrailGraded
08:14 — escalated from support

A customer emailed us. They said they found a link to what looks like our internal shipment records — customer names, emails, phone numbers, addresses — and it opened with no login.

Support confirmed the link works from their phone on mobile data. It is public.

You are the security analyst on shift. Figure out what happened and stop it.

What you need to produce

  1. The exposure closed
  2. When it started, and who caused it
  3. What was actually downloaded, and by whom
  4. A short written summary

3

Look inside the exposed bucket

Confirm it is real customer data, not a false alarm.

./.venv/bin/python -c " import sys; sys.path.insert(0,'../02-ai-security-agent') from src import aws_client s3 = aws_client.s3() for o in s3.list_objects_v2(Bucket='meridian-customer-exports')['Contents']: print(o['Key']) print() print(s3.get_object(Bucket='meridian-customer-exports', Key='exports/2026-09/shipments-customers.csv')['Body'].read().decode())"
ConfirmNames, emails, phone numbers, addresses. This is a reportable exposure.
4

Pull the logs

CloudTrail records every API call and delivers it to S3. Get it onto your machine.

./.venv/bin/python fetch_logs.py
Resultlogs/events.json — one flat list of every event, oldest first.
5

Find when it was made public, and who did it

A bucket does not become public on its own. Someone made an API call.

Look for the event that changed the bucket policy. You want the time, the username, and the source IP.

jq -r '.[] | select(.eventName=="PutBucketPolicy" or .eventName=="DeletePublicAccessBlock") | "\(.eventTime) \(.eventName) \(.userIdentity.userName) \(.sourceIPAddress) \(.requestParameters.bucketName)"' logs/events.json
Note downThe date, the username, and the IP address. You will need all three.
No jq installed?
brew install jq — or read logs/events.json in your editor and search for PutBucketPolicy.
Hint
Two events matter, four minutes apart. One removed a safety setting, the other added the public policy. The first one is why the second one worked.
6

Find out what was actually taken

Exposed is not the same as accessed. You need to know which is which.

Anonymous requests show a user identity type of AWSAccount. Successful ones have no errorCode.

jq -r '.[] | select(.eventName=="GetObject") | select(.userIdentity.type=="AWSAccount") | select(.errorCode==null) | select(.requestParameters.bucketName=="meridian-customer-exports") | "\(.eventTime) \(.sourceIPAddress) \(.requestParameters.key)"' logs/events.json
CountHow many successful downloads, how many distinct IP addresses, and which files. Those three numbers go in your answers.
CarefulNot every anonymous request succeeded, and not every request was against this bucket. Filter properly or your numbers will be wrong.
7

Decide what to do about the second public bucket

Step 2 showed two public buckets. Only one of them is a problem.

./.venv/bin/python -c " import sys; sys.path.insert(0,'../02-ai-security-agent') from src import aws_client print(aws_client.s3().get_object(Bucket='meridian-web-assets', Key='PURPOSE.txt')['Body'].read().decode())"
This is the judgement callLocking every public bucket looks decisive and takes the company website offline. Knowing which exposure is intentional is the job.
8

Close the exposure

Two things: remove the public policy, and put the safety setting back so it cannot happen again by accident.

./.venv/bin/python -c " import sys; sys.path.insert(0,'../02-ai-security-agent') from src import aws_client s3 = aws_client.s3() s3.delete_bucket_policy(Bucket='meridian-customer-exports') s3.put_public_access_block(Bucket='meridian-customer-exports', PublicAccessBlockConfiguration={'BlockPublicAcls':True,'IgnorePublicAcls':True, 'BlockPublicPolicy':True,'RestrictPublicBuckets':True}) print('closed')"
Do notDelete the objects, and do not touch the CloudTrail log bucket. You need both for the breach report.
Check it./.venv/bin/python setup.py --status
