6

Find when it was made public, and who did it

Terminal

A bucket does not go public on its own. Someone made an API call. Find it.

jq -r '.[] | select(.eventName=="PutBucketPolicy" or .eventName=="DeletePublicAccessBlock") | "\(.eventTime) \(.eventName) \(.userIdentity.userName) \(.sourceIPAddress) \(.requestParameters.bucketName)"' logs/events.json
Note down three thingsThe date, the username, and the source IP.
jq not installed?
brew install jq, or open logs/events.json in your editor and search for PutBucketPolicy.
Hint
Two events, four minutes apart, same person. One removed a safety setting; the other added the public policy. The first is why the second worked.
7

Find out what was actually downloaded

Terminal

Exposed and accessed are different things. You need to know which files really left.

jq -r '.[] | select(.eventName=="GetObject") | select(.userIdentity.type=="AWSAccount") | select(.errorCode==null) | select(.requestParameters.bucketName=="meridian-customer-exports") | "\(.eventTime) \(.sourceIPAddress) \(.requestParameters.key)"' logs/events.json
Count three thingsHow many lines (downloads), how many unique IP addresses, and how many distinct filenames.
Why each filter mattersAWSAccount means anonymous. errorCode==null means it succeeded — some attempts were denied. And the bucket filter matters because there is traffic to other buckets in the same log.

9

Close the exposure

Terminal

Remove the public policy, then put the safety setting back so it cannot happen again by accident.

./.venv/bin/python -c " import sys; sys.path.insert(0,'../02-ai-security-agent') from src import aws_client s3 = aws_client.s3() s3.delete_bucket_policy(Bucket='meridian-customer-exports') s3.put_public_access_block(Bucket='meridian-customer-exports', PublicAccessBlockConfiguration={'BlockPublicAcls':True,'IgnorePublicAcls':True, 'BlockPublicPolicy':True,'RestrictPublicBuckets':True}) print('closed')"
Do notDelete the objects, and do not touch meridian-cloudtrail-logs. You need both for the breach report.
